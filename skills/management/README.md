# Management Skills

Use this folder for management-practice skills grounded in W. Edwards Deming's work — how leadership diagnoses performance, sets policy, evaluates people, and drives continuous improvement, as distinct from `strategy/` (what to pursue) and `refinement/` (what to schedule).

> Run `system-of-profound-knowledge.md` first, and treat it alongside `product/systems-thinking-and-domain-driven-design.md` — Deming's "Appreciation for a System" and "Knowledge of Variation" are the same systems-thinking discipline applied specifically to management practice, not a separate philosophy.
>
> Historical lineage worth knowing before citing Deming here: per [Lean Manufacturing (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lean_manufacturing), Deming's post-war statistical-quality work in Japan is the direct root of Ohno and Shingo's Toyota Production System, which Womack and Jones later codified as "Lean." The [Lean Enterprise Institute](https://www.lean.org/) and [Lean Essays](https://www.leanessays.com/) (Mary Poppendieck, who first carried lean-manufacturing principles into software) are the living continuation of that lineage — useful supporting sources when a management-practice audit here touches lean/flow reasoning specifically, not just Deming's management writing.
>
> **`team-of-teams-organizational-adaptability.md` is a second, distinct management philosophy in this folder — not Deming-grounded, and not a replacement for the lens above.** Where the Deming skills audit whether a specific *practice* is sound, McChrystal's Team of Teams audits whether the *organizational structure itself* can move information and decisions fast enough for a complex, networked environment. Run both when the question is genuinely structural — they answer different questions and neither substitutes for the other.
>
> **`organizational-change-readiness-and-team-disruption-penalty.md` is a third, distinct model in this folder — also not Deming-grounded.** Where Team of Teams audits organizational structure and the Deming skills audit specific practices, this skill audits whether a *specific proposed change* (reorg, leadership rotation, tooling swap, methodology change) is being approved with its real productivity cost — grounded in Tuckman's stages of group development — actually visible, rather than treated as free. Run it whenever a change to team composition, leadership, structure, tooling, or working method is on the table, distinct from the periodic structural/practice audits above.
>
> **`situational-leadership-and-individualized-management.md` is a fourth, distinct model in this folder — also not Deming-grounded.** Where the skills above audit organizational structure, specific practices, or the cost of a specific change, this skill audits the ongoing, day-to-day relationship between a manager and each individual report — whether the manager's actual behavior (direction vs. support) is matched to that person's real, currently-diagnosed readiness for a specific task, or is instead one fixed personal style applied uniformly regardless of who's in front of them. Run it continuously as part of normal people management, not just at a change or review event.
>
> **`pdsa-improvement-cycle.md` is a cooperative, non-adversarial improvement loop — do not reach for it when the situation is actively competitive or adversarial (a competitor's move, a live incident, a negotiation).** `../strategy/ooda-loop-decision-cycle.md` (Boyd's Observe-Orient-Decide-Act) is the correct loop there: it optimizes for out-cycling an actively adapting counterpart, not for a falsifiable prediction tested at small scale against a stable system. Forcing PDSA's discipline onto a fast-moving competitive situation loses the tempo advantage; forcing OODA's speed-first discipline onto a stable internal process risks the reactive tampering `funnel-experiment.md` warns against.
>
> **`total-quality-management.md` is the organization-wide packaging the Deming-specific skills above operate inside — run it first when the question is whether a quality *system* actually exists, not just whether one practice is sound.** TQM is the named umbrella (U.S. Navy, 1985) that bundled Deming's contribution alongside Juran's, Feigenbaum's, Ishikawa's, and Crosby's into one organization-wide program; it audits customer-definition of quality, cross-departmental participation, and integration, then routes failing principles into the specific Deming tools below for mechanical diagnosis. It does not replace them.

## Purpose

These skills help leadership:

- Diagnose whether a management practice (a review cycle, a quota, a supplier policy) is sound before rolling it out or defending it.
- Distinguish system-caused outcomes from individual performance, before rating, ranking, or rewarding anyone.
- Distinguish real signals from ordinary variation, before reacting to a single data point.
- Run improvement as a disciplined, falsifiable, repeating cycle rather than a one-shot initiative or a slogan.
- Recognize the deepest, most structural failure modes (the seven diseases) that block improvement regardless of how good any single practice is.
- Assess whether the organization's structure itself (information flow, decision authority, team autonomy) can adapt fast enough for a complex, networked environment — distinct from whether any single practice is sound.
- Account for the real productivity penalty a specific organizational change imposes on a team before approving it, so work already in flight isn't put at risk without anyone having made that trade-off explicit.
- Manage each individual employee through a situational-leadership lens — diagnosing per-task readiness and matching direction/support to it — instead of applying one fixed personal management style uniformly across a whole team.

## Skills Index

- `total-quality-management.md`
  - Audits whether a quality effort is an organization-wide system (ASQ's eight principles: Customer Focus, Employee Involvement, Process Approach, Integrated System, Strategic Alignment, Continual Improvement, Fact-Based Decisions, Communications) or departmental QA under a bigger name.
  - Routes failing principles into the specific Deming skills below rather than re-deriving their mechanics.

- `system-of-profound-knowledge.md`
  - The foundational lens: Appreciation for a System, Knowledge of Variation, Theory of Knowledge, Psychology — applied together, not separately, to any management decision.
  - Routes findings into every other skill below.

- `fourteen-points-for-management.md`
  - Audits a specific practice (supplier policy, review cycle, quota, training budget) point-by-point against Deming's fourteen points.
  - Produces pass/fail per in-scope point, the specific violating mechanism, and Deming's own substitution.

- `seven-deadly-diseases.md`
  - Diagnoses organization-level patterns that block improvement structurally: short-termism, management mobility, visible-figures-only management, merit rating, and the harder-to-fix medical/liability cost diseases.
  - Ranks diseases by how directly addressable they are through management decision versus external/policy-level leverage.

- `pdsa-improvement-cycle.md` (documented under its more common name, PDCA, alongside Deming's PDSA variant)
  - Runs Plan-Do-Study-Act as the operating loop for testing any theory or change at small scale before full rollout.
  - Enforces Deming's Study-over-Check distinction: compare actual results against a stated prediction, don't just pass/fail the change. Names the Lean Enterprise Institute's differing "functionally equivalent" framing of PDCA/PDSA explicitly rather than silently picking a side.

- `red-bead-experiment.md`
  - Tests whether a performance metric used to rate or rank individuals/teams is actually bound by the shared system they work within.
  - Redirects system-bound findings from individual consequence toward system improvement.

- `funnel-experiment.md`
  - Catches tampering: reacting to a single result or normal variation by adjusting a process, policy, quota, or standard, which reliably makes a stable system worse.
  - Names the specific tampering pattern (overcorrection, chasing the last result, recalibrating off the last data point) rather than leaving it as a vague "that seems reactive."

- `team-of-teams-organizational-adaptability.md`
  - McChrystal's four pillars (Trust, Common Purpose, Shared Consciousness, Empowered Execution) as a structural-adaptability audit, distinct from the Deming-grounded skills above — checks whether the organization's structure moves information/decisions fast enough for a complex, networked environment, not whether a specific practice is well-designed.
  - Requires confirming the environment actually needs this model (genuinely complex/fast-changing) before recommending it, and a named mechanism as evidence for each pillar rather than an assumed cultural claim.

- `organizational-change-readiness-and-team-disruption-penalty.md`
  - Uses Tuckman's stages of group development (Forming/Storming/Norming/Performing/Adjourning) to name the real regression a team undergoes when its composition, leadership, structure, tooling, or working method changes — even a high-performing team is not permanently protected from reverting to an earlier, lower-productivity stage.
  - Pairs that team-level check with Prosci's ADKAR model (Awareness/Desire/Knowledge/Ability/Reinforcement) at the individual level, since a team can read as Performing while several individuals inside it are still stuck at Awareness or Desire on the specific change — a gap a team-only assessment misses.
  - Converts the regression into a concrete Little's Law throughput/cycle-time impact on the team's actual in-flight work (via `delivery/wip-limits-and-flow-protection.md`), and grounds stacked/concurrent changes directly in Prosci's change-saturation/change-fatigue research (the "at least one change project past capacity" threshold) rather than inference alone.
  - Ends every assessment in an explicit leadership decision request (approve with cost visible / defer / stage), never letting a change proceed with its cost left unstated.

- `situational-leadership-and-individualized-management.md`
  - Applies the Situational Leadership® model (Paul Hersey, 1969; The Center for Leadership Studies) to require every direct report be managed per-task, not uniformly: diagnoses Performance Readiness (Ability + Willingness) into R1-R4 and matches it to the corresponding leadership style (S1 Telling through S4 Delegating) — over-directing a ready performer reads as micromanagement, under-supporting an unready one reads as abandonment.
  - Names task specificity as the core discipline (the same person can be R4 on one responsibility and R1 on another, simultaneously — never a fixed personality label), and flags regression risk (a previously R4 person quietly falling back toward R1 after an organizational change, an unestablished trust relationship, or a lack of recognition) as a trigger for active re-diagnosis.
  - Cross-references `red-bead-experiment.md`'s warning directly: never blame an individual for a failure a mismatched leadership style actually produced.

## Suggested Usage Order

0. Use `total-quality-management.md` first when the open question is whether a genuine organization-wide quality *system* exists at all — it scopes the audit and routes into the rest of this folder for mechanics.
1. Start with `system-of-profound-knowledge.md` on any non-trivial management decision — it routes to the other five skills.
2. Use `funnel-experiment.md` and `red-bead-experiment.md` as diagnostics whenever a reaction to a result, or a comparison between people/teams, is in play — before any policy changes or ratings are finalized.
3. Use `fourteen-points-for-management.md` to audit the specific practice once the diagnostics above have run.
4. Use `seven-deadly-diseases.md` periodically (quarterly/annually) as an organization-level structural check, independent of any single decision.
5. Use `pdsa-improvement-cycle.md` to actually implement any change that survives the above — as a small-scale, falsifiable test, not a full rollout.
6. Use `team-of-teams-organizational-adaptability.md` separately, whenever the actual question is about organizational structure/adaptability rather than a specific practice — it doesn't sit in this same sequence, since it answers a different question.
7. Use `organizational-change-readiness-and-team-disruption-penalty.md` whenever a specific change to a team's composition, leadership, structure, tooling, or working method is being proposed — before it's approved, not after delivery has already slipped and the cause is unclear.
8. Use `situational-leadership-and-individualized-management.md` continuously, as part of normal one-on-ones and task assignment — not just at a review cycle or change event — and re-run it whenever step 7 flags a team that recently absorbed a disruptive change, since that's a direct trigger to re-diagnose individual readiness rather than assume it's unchanged.

## Inputs To Gather

- The specific practice, policy, target, or decision under review, and its stated intent versus its actual mechanics.
- Historical data for any metric in question, ideally plotted over time (not a single snapshot).
- Whether a control-chart-equivalent baseline exists for what counts as normal variation.
- Who is compared, rated, or rewarded by the practice, and on what basis.
- The planning/incentive time horizon currently in force (quarterly vs. multi-year).
- For a structural-adaptability question: how information/decisions currently flow, evidence for each Team of Teams pillar, and the specific complexity/speed problem in play.
- For a proposed organizational change: what's changing and which team(s) it touches, the team's current state (evidence of trust/norms/autonomy), any recent or concurrently planned disruptive changes to the same team, current in-flight work, and historical recovery-time data if available.
- For individualized management: the specific task in question, evidence of the person's Ability and Willingness for it separately, the style currently being applied, and any recent disruption (organizational change, new management relationship, lack of recognition) that could have caused readiness regression.

## Output Expectations

- A verdict on any given management decision across all four SoPK lenses, not just one.
- Explicit common-cause/special-cause classification before any reactive change is approved.
- Point-by-point and disease-by-disease audits with named mechanisms and substitutions, not generic restatements.
- Any change proposed enters a PDSA cycle at small scale with a falsifiable prediction, rather than a full rollout on belief alone.
- A four-pillar structural-adaptability assessment, with environment-fit confirmed first and the weakest pillar named with a concrete (not slogan-level) fix.
- A Tuckman-stage regression assessment and Little's Law-quantified in-flight-work impact for any proposed organizational change, ending in an explicit leadership decision request rather than a silent green light.
- A per-person, per-task Performance Readiness diagnosis with a matched leadership style, an explicit mismatch flag where the current style doesn't fit, and a regression-risk check rather than an assumed carry-forward of a past diagnosis.
